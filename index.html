What we are not

We should be honest about
what quantum computers do.

The quantum computing field has spent a decade overpromising. There is a narrow set of problems where quantum hardware offers genuine advantage. Everything else is better handled by classical compute.

We built Tensir for the narrow set.

× Protein folding and molecular biology at scale AlphaFold does this better
× Supply chain and logistics optimization Classical heuristics win here
× Financial portfolio optimization No quantum advantage demonstrated
× Breaking encryption via Shor's algorithm Not on any near-term timeline
× Database search acceleration via Grover Quadratic speedup, limited practical impact

The actual problem

A small class of problems.
Genuinely intractable.

Some molecular systems have electrons that refuse to behave independently. Classical approximations break down mathematically — not because computers are too slow, but because the problem is structurally intractable.

Partially filled d and f orbitals. Strongly correlated electrons. The physics community has known this ceiling for decades.

"This is a small, specific, deeply unglamorous class of problems. Most quantum hype ignores it entirely. We do not."

Why classical compute fails

The methods are known.
The ceiling is real.

Classical methods work well — until correlated electrons. Then they hit a hard mathematical ceiling that more compute will not move.

DFT

Density Functional Theory. Efficient and widely used. Fails on strong correlation — the exchange-correlation functional is an approximation with known systematic errors on d and f systems.

CCSD(T)

Gold standard for weakly correlated systems. Scales as O(N⁷). Becomes computationally prohibitive at the system sizes that matter for real industrial materials.

DMRG

The best classical tool for strongly correlated systems. Still exponentially hard for the 3D geometries that dominate industrial materials problems.

This is not a hardware limitation. The mathematics is the ceiling. Quantum hardware addresses the problem at the level of the physics — native representation of quantum states, not brute force.

Quantum algorithms

The algorithms exist.
Running them is expensive.

VQE

Variational Quantum Eigensolver

Hybrid quantum-classical optimization of a parameterized circuit. Variational parameters are learnable — each run informs the next. The optimization landscape is the asset.

QPE

Quantum Phase Estimation

Fault-tolerant ground state energy estimation. Higher accuracy than VQE on sufficient hardware. Calibration decisions and circuit architecture compound across experiments.

Every run costs real money. Calibration drifts across hardware families. Shot budgets matter. Every insight your team generates disappears unless you capture it. Most R&D teams relearn the same lessons run after run.
